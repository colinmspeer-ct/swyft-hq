## Method and limits

- **VERIFIED** means the page was loaded with WebFetch and the excerpt was taken from the returned page text. **SNIPPET-ONLY** means the page blocked fetching (BBB.org, ConsumerAffairs and G2 all returned HTTP 403), so the text comes only from search-result snippets or summaries. Check these again by hand before relying on them.
- Excerpts in quotation marks are verbatim and under 25 words. Everything else is paraphrase and is marked "(paraphrase)". Reviewer names are left out.
- Dates are review posting dates as shown on the page.
- **Reddit could not be reached.** The search tool refuses reddit.com ("domain not accessible to our user agent"), so no Reddit threads are included. If Reddit evidence is needed, someone has to pull it by hand.
- Several sources are vendors or competitors (PestNet, On The Map, BizIQ). They are marked as such and should count as weaker evidence.
- Where a source covers mixed trades, the industry tag shows what the reviewer stated. "n/s" means not stated.

## Theme synthesis: by service area

Each theme is the objection a buyer might raise, followed by the items that support it.

### 1. Door-to-door sales teams
1. **"Will your reps misrepresent the contract or say whatever it takes to close?"** The verbal pitch doesn't match the paperwork (one-time vs. recurring, 10-day vs. 3-day cancel window). Items 51, 53, 56.
2. **"Will pushy reps hurt my brand and reviews?"** High-pressure tactics, "half truths", "promises you the world". Items 53, 54, 58.
3. **"Will customers sign up and then cancel, leaving me with churn and chargebacks?"** Early-termination fees of $100-$250 cause anger and bad reviews. Items 52, 55, 57.
4. **"Are your reps compliant with permits and local solicitation rules?"** A state AG action covered missing permits, no contract copies, and the 3-day right not disclosed. Item 50.
5. **"Do customers actually understand what they're signing?"** Late-night tablet signatures, small print, no copy in the customer portal. Items 51, 52.
6. **"Are reps trained, or just summer kids?"** Items 58, 54.
7. **"Will 'free trial' or 'first month free' pitches backfire?"** Items 54, 56, 58 (compare the SaaS version in item 19).

### 2. Cold calling / appointment setting
1. **"Will the appointments actually be qualified?"** Items 41, 46, 47.
2. **"Are the appointments real, or padded/fabricated?"** Items 42, 47.
3. **"What if I pay for months and get nothing? Is there a refund or out?"** Items 43, 45.
4. **"Will your callers sound like they know my business?"** Untrained agents posing as the company, quality that depends on the team. Items 44, 48.
5. **"Will no-shows and ghosting waste my techs' time?"** Items 45, 47.
6. **"Will aggressive calling make my company look spammy?"** Items 47, 49.
7. **"Will I be pressured into signing?"** Item 46.
8. **"How will I know what's happening? Is there call-level reporting?"** Items 48, 29 (cross-cutting).

## Theme synthesis: by industry (only where evidence exists)

### Pest control
1. D2D horror stories (misrepresentation, cancel fees, compliance) have made homeowners wary, so pest owners worry that hired reps will damage their reputation. Items 50-58.
2. Agencies lock pest companies into long contracts and keep the website. Items 7, 8, 29, 30.
3. Leads get shared across several pest companies. Item 29.
4. Reporting focuses on vanity metrics instead of calls and booked jobs. Item 29.

### Lawn care
Evidence is thin; no lawn-specific owner reviews were verified.
1. Auto-renewal clauses with long notice windows. Item 31.
2. Lead marketplaces send wrong-service leads, e.g. lawn mower repair to a handyman. Item 39.
3. Seasonality: paying a year-round contract for a seasonal business. Item 31 (vendor). An inference, weakly supported.

### HVAC (and the closely related plumbing / ServiceTitan shops)
1. Charged for irrelevant calls (chiropractors, dentists). Item 3.
2. Inflated revenue attribution in agency dashboards. Item 3.
3. Integration promises (ServiceTitan) not delivered, with no way out. Item 32.
4. Account-manager churn, and Google results worse than Facebook. Item 12 (plumbing).

### Cleaning
1. Charged for leads the business never responded to. Item 40.
2. Shared and unresponsive leads on marketplaces. Items 40, 34.
3. (SNIPPET-ONLY, not logged as an item) A cleaning startup reportedly got a collections letter from Angi without gaining a customer. Unverified; do not use without manual check.

### Pool
No verifiable negative reviews of pool-specific marketing vendors were found. Results were mostly vendor pages and positive testimonials. Don't build pool-specific FAQ claims on this log; use the general themes instead.
